## Timeline overview

|------|------|------|-----------------|
| **Birth and upbringing** | Nov 13, 1964 | Born in Weymouth, Dorset, England | a dual cultural background — born British, raised American |
| | 1968 | Moved with family to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, USA | grew up in an American industrial city |
| | High school | Started writing code | early exposure to programming laid the hacker groundwork |

| **Harvard PhD** | 1988 | Earned an MS from Harvard (computer science) | |
| | 1990 | Earned a PhD from Harvard (computer science) | did Lisp-related research at Harvard, laying the groundwork for his deep understanding of Lisp and his core belief that "programming languages shape thought" |

| | 1996 | Viaweb went live | started on a $10,000 seed investment from a lawyer friend, turning down VCs; this early bootstrapping experience shaped YC's later emphasis on frugality |

| **Interim period/pivot to writing** | 1999-2001 | After leaving Yahoo, tried to keep painting, gradually shifted toward writing | realized he had more talent and passion for writing than for painting. "Do what you love" isn't a platitude for him — it was a real turning point in his life |
| **The essay-writing boom** | 2001 | Began publishing essays on paulgraham.com; unveiled the Arc language for the first time at the LL1 conference (after only 3 weeks of development) | pioneered the genre of "programmers writing long essays." | | 2003 | Published "Hackers and Painters" — the hacker/painter analogy | |
| | 2003 | Published "A Plan for Spam" — proposing Bayesian spam filtering | this essay directly shaped the technical direction of the entire anti-spam industry |

| | Mar 2005 | Gave the "How to Start a Startup" talk at the Harvard Computer Society | this talk directly gave rise to the founding of YC |
| **Founding YC** | Mar 11, 2005 | Came up with the idea for YC while walking with Jessica Livingston | Jessica was working in investment banking at the time but didn't like it; PG's ideas and her execution ability complemented each other perfectly |

## Key intellectual turning points

| Turning point | Trigger event | Before | After |
|--------|---------|------|------|
| **From philosophy to CS** | Cornell philosophy BA -> Harvard CS PhD | purely humanistic thinking | a cross-disciplinary lens of philosophy + programming, keeping him focused on "why" rather than just "how" |
| **From academia to painting** | went to RISD and Florence after the PhD | an academic path | came to understand how a maker works: not planning first and executing second, but discovering while doing |
| **From painting to founding a company** | founded Viaweb | an artist's identity | an entrepreneur's identity, but retaining a maker's working philosophy |
| **From founding a company to writing** | left Yahoo | a person who does things | a person who thinks and writes. Discovered the essay was his true medium |
| **From personal writing to institution-building** | founded YC | an independent thinker | tried to systematize and scale his own startup experience into something teachable |
| **From institution-building back to the individual** | stepped down from YC | an organizational manager | returned to being an independent thinker. Recognized he wasn't suited to managing an organization's growth |
| **From Silicon Valley to England** | moved to England in 2016 | at the center of Silicon Valley | physical distance brought intellectual distance. Getting away from the noise made his essays deeper, slower, and more fundamental |
| **A new position in the AI era** | the 2024-2026 AI wave | focused on startups and programming | began thinking deeply about AI's effect on writing, thinking, taste, and entrepreneurship. Core view: in the AI era, taste beats execution, and the ability to write equals the ability to think |
