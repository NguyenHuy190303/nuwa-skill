## 1. Personality analysis from the psychology field

### 1.1 The Dangerous Case of Donald Trump series

**Source**: edited by Bandy X. Lee, across multiple editions (the 2017 original with 27
experts, a 2019 update with 37, the 2024 *More Dangerous Case* with 40, and the 2025 *Much
More Dangerous Case* with 50 experts)

**Core argument**:

Several psychiatrists and mental-health experts believe Trump displays a combination of the
following personality traits:
- Narcissistic Personality Disorder
- Antisocial Personality traits
- Paranoid Personality
- Malignant narcissism — a combination of narcissism + antisocial traits + paranoia + sadistic
  tendencies

Psychologist Philip Zimbardo (designer of the Stanford Prison Experiment) and Rosemary Sword
explain Trump's impulsivity through "unbridled and extreme present hedonism."

Craig Malkin describes the combination of malignant narcissism and power as a "lethal
combination."

**The "shared psychosis" concept**:
Bandy X. Lee proposes that Trump's psychological state is "contagious" — spreading outward
from a center of power, first infecting vulnerable individuals, ultimately affecting an entire
group, so that his followers collectively detach from reality even though no individual
follower is necessarily mentally ill.

**A major controversy — the Goldwater Rule**:
- the American Psychiatric Association (APA) explicitly bars psychiatrists from diagnosing a
  public figure they haven't personally examined
- Allen Frances (one of the principal drafters of the DSM-5's personality-disorder criteria)
  explicitly disagrees: he argues Trump doesn't meet the threshold for a personality-disorder
  diagnosis, because diagnosis requires "clinically significant distress or impairment," and
  Trump himself isn't distressed — he causes distress in others
- Frances believes this kind of criticism stigmatizes people with real mental illness
- an article in the academic journal Tandfonline criticizes this line of analysis as
  "misdiagnosing Trump"

**Source links**: [Scientific American - Shared Psychosis](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-shared-psychosis-of-donald-trump-and-his-loyalists/) | [a JAAPL book review](https://jaapl.org/content/46/2/267) | [a critical article at The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/trump-accused-of-malignant-narcissism-but-how-accurately-can-you-diagnose-someone-youve-never-met-242277)

### 1.2 The Strange Case of Donald J. Trump: A Psychological Reckoning

**Source**: Dan P. McAdams, Henry Wade Rogers Professor of Psychology at Northwestern
University, Oxford University Press, 2020

**Core concept: "the episodic man"**

Rather than pursuing a diagnosis, McAdams proposes a distinctive framework from personality
science:

Trump is a rare person who lacks an inner story. A normal person develops a coherent life
narrative that gives life meaning, unity, and a moral framework; Trump treats life as a series
of isolated campaigns — each episode is independent, forms no arc, and accumulates no meaning.

The core inference:
- **"Truth for Donald Trump is whatever works to win in the moment"**
- he enjoys enormous freedom from moral and normative constraint, because what he said and did
  in the previous episode doesn't bind the next one
- this explains why his contradictory statements cause him no distress at all

**Source**: [a Northwestern Now overview](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2020/03/the-strange-case-of-donald-j-trump-a-psychological-reckoning) | [a PMC academic article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8763223/)

### 1.3 Academic research: the link between narcissism and voting behavior

**Source**: a PMC academic paper, "Dimensions of pathological narcissism and intention to vote

narcissism and the intention to support Trump, providing empirical data for understanding his

## 2. Internal observations from family

### 2.1 Too Much and Never Enough: How My Family Created the World's Most Dangerous Man

**Source**: Mary L. Trump (a clinical psychologist, Trump's niece), Simon & Schuster, July
2020. Sold over 1 million copies on its first day.

**An analysis of the family dynamics**:

Mary Trump diagnoses her grandfather, Fred Trump Sr., as a "high-functioning sociopath," and
considers him the central force that shaped Trump:
- Fred Sr. treated weakness as an unforgivable flaw
- Trump's mother was hospitalized for six months when he was two, leaving him without
  maternal care during a critical emotional period
- The result: Trump never built an internal model for empathy, vulnerability, or reciprocal
  relationships

**Her diagnostic claim about Trump** (based on the DSM-5):
Mary Trump believes Trump meets all nine criteria for Narcissistic Personality Disorder, and
may also have:
- antisocial personality traits
- dependent personality traits
- a learning disability affecting emotion, behavior, and cognition

**A core insight**: Trump's father created an environment where "praise was the only
currency," never giving real love — only a sense of worth when winning. This explains why
Trump's fear of failure reaches a pathological level.

**Note**: this is a family memoir, and personal grievance (involving an inheritance dispute)
is a factor — it should be cross-verified against other sources.

**Source**: [an NPR book review](https://www.npr.org/2020/07/23/894159895/trumps-niece-describes-a-toxic-family-dynamic-where-kindness-was-weakness) | [a Psychology Today analysis](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/narcissism-demystified/202007/provocative-psychological-analysis-trump-trump)

## 3. Memoirs from former White House staff

### 3.1 Bob Woodward's Fear: Trump in the White House (2018) and Rage (2020)

**Source**: Bob Woodward, a veteran investigative journalist (a Pulitzer winner, the Watergate
reporter), based on hundreds of hours of primary interviews, meeting notes, private diaries,
and documents

**Key findings — staff's private assessments of Trump**:

- White House Chief of Staff John Kelly: called Trump an "idiot" and "unhinged"
- Defense Secretary James Mattis: said Trump's comprehension was on par with "a fifth or sixth
  grader"
- senior staff removed a draft document from the Oval Office desk to prevent the president
  from signing an order that could have endangered a classified intelligence operation —
  Woodward calls this an "administrative coup d'état"

**Recorded behavior patterns**:
- refuses to read briefing material before deciding
- ignores and belittles expert advice
- runs the Oval Office on intuition and snap decisions

**Source**: [Fear - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear:_Trump_in_the_White_House)

### 3.2 John Bolton's The Room Where It Happened: A White House Memoir (2020)

**Source**: John Bolton, former National Security Advisor (2018-2019), Simon & Schuster

**Core description**:

Bolton found that Trump treated foreign policy like a real-estate deal — about personal
relationships, manufacturing TV moments, and advancing his own interests.

Key observations:
- **"I am hard-pressed to identify any significant Trump decision during my tenure that wasn't
  driven by reelection calculations."**
- Trump asked Chinese President Xi Jinping to buy more soybeans and wheat to secure
  agricultural-state electoral support — treating state diplomacy as a campaign tool
- Trump's admiration for autocrats often led him to make unilateral concessions in
  negotiations, weakening America's negotiating position

**How foreign leaders manipulated Trump**: Bolton describes specific tactics foreign leaders
and their staffs used to manipulate Trump, especially how his admiration for "strongmen"
clouded his judgment.

**Note**: Bolton is a hawk who deeply disagreed with Trump on policies like Syria, so his
criticism carries a political tinge — but his position gives his observations internal
authority.

**Source**: [an NPR book review](https://www.npr.org/2020/06/17/879609378/john-bolton-unloads-on-former-boss-trump-even-if-its-a-little-late-now) | [key revelations from CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/john-bolton-book-trump-revelations-the-room-where-it-happened/)

### 3.3 Cassidy Hutchinson's Enough (2023)

**Source**: Cassidy Hutchinson, former assistant to White House Chief of Staff Mark Meadows,
Simon & Schuster, September 2023

**Specific records of Trump's angry behavior**:

- on December 1, 2020, on learning Attorney General William Barr had publicly stated no
  evidence of election fraud had been found, Trump threw his lunch plate against the White
  House dining-room wall, splattering ketchup across it
- on other occasions he "flipped the tablecloth, sending everything on the table crashing to
  the floor"
- on the day of the January 6 incident, knowing some people carrying weapons had failed the
  security screening, he still insisted they be let in, and had a heated clash with the Secret
  Service

Hutchinson describes Trump's "terrifying temper," and says seeing firsthand that it wasn't
just performance or a show for the cameras made her regret joining the West Wing.

**Source**: [Enough - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_(book))

### 3.5 John Kelly's public disclosures (2024)

**Source**: former White House Chief of Staff, retired four-star general John Kelly,
interviewed by The New York Times and The Atlantic, October 2024

**Shocking recorded private remarks**:

- Trump reportedly said more than once that "Hitler did some good things, too"
- Trump said "I need the kind of generals that Hitler had"
- Kelly believes Trump "meets the definition of a fascist," preferring "a dictator's

**Background**: these remarks reportedly occurred during the 2020 George Floyd protests, when
Trump was frustrated that generals, under a democratic system, couldn't be ordered to carry
out illegal actions.

**Note**: Trump's camp denies these characterizations, calling Kelly a disgruntled figure
fired from his job. These are unrecorded personal recollections, but they come from the most

**Source**: [NBC News reporting](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2024-election/john-kelly-says-donald-trump-meets-definition-fascist-rcna176706) | [CNN reporting](https://www.cnn.com/2024/10/22/politics/trump-fascist-john-kelly/)

## 4. Assessments from key collaborators

### 4.1 Tony Schwartz — the ghostwriter of The Art of the Deal

**Source**: Tony Schwartz, the actual author of The Art of the Deal (1987), published in 1987,
the book that established Trump's public image

**His assessment after spending 18 months with Trump**:

- "writing that book is my greatest regret," and he wishes the book "had never been published"
- **"Trump was not just willing to lie, he was comfortable doing it, without a shred of guilt
  or remorse"**
- "there's a hollowness at Trump's core. A lack of soul. A lack of a heart."
- describes Trump as "sociopathically impulsive, self-centered," with an extremely short
  attention span, leading to "an astonishing degree of superficial knowledge and ignorance"
- if he rewrote it, he'd title the book *The Sociopath*

**Background**: Schwartz took the ghostwriting job for the money, and later publicly repented,
continuing to criticize Trump on social media, with a clear adversarial stance — but his
first-hand observations are unique.

**Source**: [CBS News reporting](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tony-schwartz-trumps-ghostwriter-says-writing-the-art-of-the-deal-is-the-greatest-regret-of-his-life/)

## 5. Foreign leaders' perspectives and coping strategies

### 5.1 Angela Merkel — Freedom: Memoirs 1954-2021 (2024)

**Source**: former German Chancellor Angela Merkel, memoir published December 2024

**Her direct description of Trump**:
- Trump "clearly fascinated by Putin," captivated by politicians with autocratic tendencies
- **"there was always a kind of fascination with the sheer power of what these people could
  do"**
- Trump dreams of bypassing parliamentary institutions and making decisions alone — "you
  cannot reconcile that with democratic values"
- describes Trump as having "a real-estate agent's mindset, focused on his own interests"

**Note**: the overall assessment of Merkel's memoir is that it's information-rich but not
deeply analytical, with no major new exclusive revelations, and phrased fairly restrainedly.

**Source**: [CNN reporting](https://www.cnn.com/2024/12/03/world/angela-merkel-trump-fascination-putin-kim-intl/) | [a Washington Post book review](https://www.washingtonpost.com/books/2024/11/29/angela-merkel-freedom-memoir-review/)

### 5.2 Putin's manipulation strategy

**Source**: analysis from a former CIA Moscow station chief (an NPR interview), reports from
several intelligence analysts

Putin's specific tactics toward Trump:
- **flattery**: Putin understood Trump's ego and insecurities deeply, and used praise as a
  tool
- a psychological game: Putin used a combination of flattery and deception with every US
  president he dealt with over two decades
- an interesting paradox: flattery is, to Putin, a sign of weakness — yet he turns around and
  uses it to manipulate Trump

Observers of the Helsinki summit (2018): Trump displayed an "almost deferential" posture
toward Putin at the press conference; no one knows the real content of their one-on-one
meeting.

Former National Security Advisor H.R. McMaster describes how Putin "played" Trump's ego:
**"Putin understood how to manipulate Trump's ego."**

**Source**: [an NPR analysis](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5580313) | [CNN reporting on world leaders' flattery](https://www.cnn.com/2017/05/04/politics/trump-foreign-leaders-flattery/index.html)

### 5.3 Shinzo Abe's "managing Trump" strategy

**Source**: foreign-policy observers, Asian diplomatic analysis (Foreign Affairs)

Abe was the most successful example among allied leaders at "managing Trump":
- flew to Trump Tower immediately after the election, becoming the first foreign leader to
  request a meeting
- heavily praised Trump's golf game
- built a buffer through sustained flattery and personal rapport, protecting Japan's
  substantive interests

The core logic: Trump values personal relationships over policy content, so maintaining a
personal emotional bond is an effective diplomatic strategy.

**Source**: [a Foreign Affairs analysis](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/asia/asias-trump-problem)

## 6. Political scientists' systematic analysis of Trumpism

### 6.1 Steven Levitsky & Daniel Ziblatt: a framework for how democracies die

**Source**: Harvard political-science professors, *How Democracies Die* (2018), plus a 2025
updated analysis article

The core framework:
- modern authoritarianism doesn't arrive through a coup, but through **steadily eroding
  political norms and democratic institutions**
- Levitsky and Lucan Way (2025) argue: the US faces the risk of "competitive
  authoritarianism" — elections continue to be held, but the machinery of the state is
  weaponized against the opposition
- a survey of over 500 political scientists (April 2025): a large majority believe the US is
  transitioning rapidly from a liberal democracy toward authoritarianism

**Source**: [an in-depth Foreign Affairs article](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/path-american-authoritarianism-trump) | [NPR reporting](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/22/nx-s1-5340753/trump-democracy-authoritarianism-competive-survey-political-scientist)

### 6.2 The social-psychological basis of Trumpism (a profile of his supporters)

**Source**: a synthesis of several academic studies (Psychology Today, Imperial College
Business School, Berkeley, New Lines Magazine, and others)

**Who is drawn to Trump? The intersection of three core factors**:

1. **The authoritarian personality**:
   - rigid adherence to in-group norms
   - deference to authority that reinforces those norms
   - strong hostility toward challengers and "outsiders"
   - in the 2016 Republican primary, authoritarian-attitude scores were the strongest
     statistical predictor of support for Trump

2. **The economics-neuroticism combination**:
   - Imperial College research: Trump won only in areas with high neuroticism, economic
     deprivation, and low ethnic diversity simultaneously
   - neuroticism is a personality trait characterized by anxiety, fear, and anger
   - people who feel threatened by demographic change, economic decline, and negative emotion
     tend to support authoritarian leaders

3. **Social dominance orientation**:
   - Trump activates social dominance orientation by repeatedly emphasizing group-status
     distinctions (white people vs. immigrants/minorities)
   - the narrative frame: "I'm the disruptor, I'm here to drain the swamp, I'm here to blow up
     Washington" — appeals to anyone dissatisfied with the status quo

**The emotional mechanism**: an authoritarian personality is "activated" under a sense of
threat, and a scapegoat narrative frames a target group as both an economic and a survival
threat.

**Source**: [the full Psychology Today analysis](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/mind-in-the-machine/201812/complete-psychological-analysis-trumps-support) | [the Imperial College Business School study](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/ib-knowledge/management/neuroticism-deprivation-and-racial-bias-trumps-unique-authoritarian-appeal/)

## 7. A summary of behavior patterns: core regularities observed from outside

### 7.1 Psychological buttons (exploited weaknesses)

| Weakness | Concrete manifestation | Source evidence |
|------|---------|---------|
| Ego needs | flattery instantly shifts his negotiating position (the Abe, Putin cases) | Bolton, Woodward, CNN reporting |
| Extreme sensitivity to criticism | treats any negative coverage as a personal attack that must be countered | Grisham, Schwartz |
| An obsession with "winning" | willing to concede on details, as long as he can call it "a win" | McAdams, Bolton |
| Fear of failure | the degree of his psychological collapse after the 2020 election | Hutchinson, Meadows |
| A short attention span | loses focus on negotiation detail once flattered and entertained | Schwartz, Grisham |

### 7.2 The gap between private and public behavior

**Areas with a documented contrast**:
- **Public**: rude, provocative, extremely performative; **private** (per some close aides):
  sometimes generous, thanks subordinates, occasionally shows personal warmth
- **But**: multiple independent records show equally intense rage in private (the ketchup
  incident, flipping the table)
- private recordings show "he is equally narcissistic, unstable, and uninformed in private and
  in public" (per Woodward's recordings)
- publicly claims to support someone, privately expresses contempt — for example, publicly
  praising Vanessa Guillén as a hero, while privately complaining "not worth 60 grand for a
  damn Mexican" (per Kelly's disclosure)

### 7.3 The operating logic of his transactional personality

**Core transactional logic** (source: an MIT Press academic analysis + psychological
research):
- find an opponent's weakness, maximize the leverage from it
- before a contract is signed, any tactic is acceptable (including deception)
- breaching a contract afterward is acceptable if the cost-benefit works out
- personal relationships are also transactions — "I give you something, you give me
  something," with no loyalty involved

**The "generosity-punishment" cycle for manipulating others** (a 2025 analysis):
first inflict harm through tariffs, budget cuts, or withdrawn aid — enough that the other side
notices — then generously "temporarily withdraw" or "ease" the harm, creating dependency and
keeping the other side perpetually in a manipulable state.

## 8. A map of disagreement and controversy

### Controversy 1: can a public figure be diagnosed remotely?

| Position | Representative | View |
|------|---------|------|
| In favor of remote assessment | Bandy X. Lee, John Gartner | "the duty to warn" outweighs the Goldwater Rule |
| Opposed to remote diagnosis | Allen Frances (a principal author of the DSM-5) | doesn't meet the diagnostic threshold; stigmatizes mental illness |
| A middle position | an analysis from The Conversation/City St George's, University of London | behavior can be described, but no diagnostic conclusion can be drawn |

### Controversy 2: the real face of his private behavior

| Angle | Description | Source |
|------|------|------|
| A positive account (a loyal supporter's view) | generous, loyal, thoughtful, "the dearest person" in private | some long-serving aides |
| A negative account (a departed staffer's view) | a terrifying temper, contempt for subordinates, private remarks sharply contradicting the public ones | Kelly, Hutchinson, Grisham, Woodward |
| Recorded evidence | equally narcissistic and unstable in private and in public | Woodward's recordings |

### Controversy 3: is Trumpism equivalent to fascism?

| Position | Representative | View |
|------|---------|------|
| Yes | John Kelly, Timothy Snyder, Jason Stanley | meets the key elements of the definition of fascism |
| No/use with caution | some historians | fundamentally different from historical fascism; misusing the concept weakens analysis |
| The "competitive authoritarianism" frame | Levitsky & Way | a more precise contemporary model of democratic backsliding than "fascism" |
