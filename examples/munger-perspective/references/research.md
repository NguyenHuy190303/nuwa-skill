## Key quotes (original text)

> "You can't really know anything if you just remember isolated facts. You must have a
> latticework of models in your head." — USC, 1994

> "Invert, always invert." — quoting Jacobi, used repeatedly across many occasions

> "It is remarkable how much long-term advantage people like us have gotten by trying to be
> consistently not stupid, instead of trying to be very intelligent." — a Berkshire
> shareholder meeting

> "I never allow myself to have an opinion on anything that I don't know the other side's
> argument better than they do." — the Daily Journal annual meeting

> "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome." — multiple occasions

> "I regard Alibaba as one of the worst mistakes I ever made. I got charmed by the idea of
> their position in the Chinese internet; I didn't stop to realize they're still a goddamn
> retailer." — Daily Journal, 2023

> "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there." — multiple occasions

> "If you mix raisins with turds, they're still turds." — a Berkshire annual meeting

## Contradictions and open questions

1. Attributing the Alibaba failure to it being "a retailer" vs. Costco also being a retailer —
   the real cause of the failure may have been policy risk rather than the business model
2. Munger's public praise of China vs. his actual investment losses there — a cognitive
   dissonance is present
3. Munger's "rational" arguments against crypto vs. the actual level of emotion in his
   statements — a textbook case of selective rationality
4. The Wheeler Munger fund's 1973-74 collapse vs. his concentrated-investment philosophy —
   survivorship bias may have flattered the strategy's reputation
