## III. Core frameworks and formulas (actionable)

### 3.1 The topic-selection system: the Endless Idea Generator

**Source**: [Dickie Bush on X](https://x.com/dickiebush/status/1577293300570247168) (primary) + [Ship 30 Idea Generator](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-generate-112-new-content-ideas-in-30-minutes) (primary)

A four-step process, claimed to produce 100+ topics in 30 minutes:

**Step 1: The 2-Year Test (find your topics)**
- Ask yourself: what problems did I solve over the last two years? What did I learn?
- Freely list every answer without judging them
- Converge to 3-5 topic buckets
- Pick the 3 that excite you most

**Step 2: Add Specificity (add constraints)**
- Add specific constraints to a topic until it's "uncomfortably specific," then add one more layer
- Dimensions to constrain by: industry, audience, geography, platform, price point, distribution channel
- Principle: "the constraint you add should turn your target reader into yourself, two years ago"
- Example: money -> investing -> investing in your 20s -> investing in your 20s with no financial background

**Step 3: 4A Framework × Proven Approaches (generate a headline matrix)**

4A represents content angles:
| Angle | Meaning | Common formats |
|------|------|----------|
| **Actionable** | teaches the reader how to do something | Tips, Hacks, Resources, Guides, How-to |
| **Analytical** | backed by data/analysis | Stats, Trends, Reasons, Numbers, Frameworks |
| **Aspirational** | inspiration + possibility | Lessons, Mistakes, Habits, Reflections |
| **Anthropological** | touches on universal human nature | Fears, Failures, Lies, Struggles |

Example usage — topic "building a daily writing habit":
- Actionable: "X mistakes to avoid when building a daily writing habit"
- Analytical: "Why making it to Day X is the key to building a writing habit"
- Aspirational: "3 lessons I learned from writing for 600 days in a row"
- Anthropological: "The #1 reason people stop writing shortly after starting"

**Step 4: Choose 3 Ideas (pick and execute)**
- Pick the 3 from the matrix that excite you most
- Start writing — "when you sit down to write the topic that lit you up, it feels effortless"

**Key insight**: one topic × 4A × multiple formats = hundreds of pieces of content. Topic selection isn't a matter of inspiration — it's a matter of combinatorics.

### 3.2 The headline formula: Headline Architecture

**Source**: [5 Pillars of Digital Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) + [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

**Every headline must answer three questions**:
1. WHO — who is this written for?
2. WHAT — what's it about?
3. WHY — why should the reader read it? (the promise/benefit)

**A 5-element checklist**:
1. How many? (a number creates a container)
2. WHAT? (precise word choice)
3. WHO? (the narrower the better)
4. FEEL (the emotional tone)
5. Outcome/Promise (what the reader gets)

**Headline Checklist (5 progressive steps)**:
1. Be CLEAR, not Clever — no wordplay, say what it's about directly
2. Specify the WHAT — use precise terms (save vs. invest vs. make money are different things)
3. Specify the WHO — narrow it until it's uncomfortable (e.g. "junior accountants at healthcare software companies")
4. Specify the WHY — be explicit about what the reader gets
5. Twist The Knife — add one more layer of payoff/surprise

**10 high-performing headline formats**:
1. A big number ("I studied 1,000...")
2. A dollar sign ("How I turned $100 into...")
3. A credible name ("Warren Buffett's...")
4. "Just happened" ("This just happened...")
5. A question ("Why do most writers fail?")
6. A success story ("How I went from X to Y")
7. An unexpected combination ("What poker taught me about writing")
8. Industry-specific ("For SaaS founders...")
9. A nested twist ("The writing mistake that kills your investing")
10. An X-item list ("7 ways to...")

**Progressive Intensification Pattern**:
- Base: "How to generate 100 ideas"
- + a time constraint: "in 30 minutes"
- + overcoming an obstacle: "even if you think you have nothing to say"

### 3.3 The thread-writing formula

**Source**: [How to Write a Twitter Thread](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-a-twitter-thread) (primary) + [Nicolas Cole 7 Templates Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary)

#### The four-part structure: Lead-In → Main Points → TL;DR → CTA

**1. The Lead-In Tweet (the opening hook)**
Must answer:
- Who is this written for?
- What's it about?
- Why should I trust you?
- What will the reader get out of it?

Hook formula structure:
- A credibility element ("One of the most legendary...")
- A time/scenario anchor ("In 1982...")
- The core benefit ("How to write...")
- A concrete deliverable ("10 bullets on effective writing")

**Pro tip**: top creators write 10-15 versions of the hook before publishing

**2. Main Points (the body)**
- Each tweet's first sentence serves as a mini-headline
- Use bullet points instead of paragraphs
- The 1/3/1 rhythm: one opening sentence -> three points/examples -> one transition sentence
- Every tweet must be able to stand alone as a complete piece
- Sweet spot: 5-7 tweets (not counting the closing CTA)

**3. The TL;DR Recap**
- Write the TL;DR first, as an outline
- List only the headline of each main point
- Lets the reader skim the whole thing at a glance

**4. The CTA (call to action)**
- Summarize the core takeaway
- Reinforce the value already delivered
- Tell the reader explicitly what to do next
- Template: "If you enjoyed this thread, follow me for more like this"
- Cole's analogy: the CTA is the "gift shop" — after the reader tours the exhibit, you guide them to the shop by the exit

#### Nicolas Cole's 7 thread templates

**Source**: [Nicolas Cole Tweet](https://x.com/Nicolascole77/status/1483922200662970370) (primary) + [Typeshare Templates](https://typeshare.co/templates/pack/nicolas-cole-thread-hooks) (primary tool)

Cole claims his 200+ threads use essentially only these 7 templates. The confirmed ones are:

1. **Framework Thread** — formula: To solve X [a hard problem], I do Y [an unconventional approach], To achieve Z [a desirable result]
2. **Story Thread** — structure: the ending -> the beginning -> "read through to find the middle"
3. **Actionable Takeaways Thread** — a checklist, each item giving one executable piece of advice
4. **Curation Thread** — "I've read everything {creator} has done for the last {timeframe}. These are the best threads on {topic}."
5. **Lessons Learned Thread** — lessons extracted from personal experience
6. **Mistakes Thread** — "X mistakes I made doing Y"
7. **Contrarian Thread** — challenges a common assumption

(Note: the full details of all 7 templates come from Cole's original Twitter thread; some template names here are inferred from Ship 30's teaching content and the Typeshare template pack)

#### Three high-performing thread types

**Source**: [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

1. **Stories** — give the ending first -> then the beginning -> the reader reads through to find the "middle"
2. **Frameworks** — a curated or original system of thinking, with a clear benefit to the reader
3. **Actionable Takeaways** — a checklist for achieving a specific result

### 3.4 The formatting-rhythm system

**Source**: [5 Pillars](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-5-pillars-of-digital-writing) (primary) + [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

#### The 1/3/1 writing rhythm

Core idea: "If your writing isn't skimmable, it isn't readable."

**The 1/3/1 structure**:
- 1 hook sentence to open
- 3 sentences of expansion (pulling the reader in deeper)
- 1 transition/summary sentence

Variants: 1/4/1, 1/5/1, 1/2/5/2/1 (like a musical crescendo -> decrescendo)

**Why it works**: single-sentence lines act as "checkpoints," giving readers a small dopamine hit that encourages them to keep reading

#### Six types of paragraph openers

1. A strong declarative statement
2. A thought-provoking question
3. A counterintuitive opinion
4. A moment in time
5. A vulnerable confession
6. A strange/unique insight

#### Core formatting principles
- Convert paragraphs into bullet lists
- Open each section with a single sentence
- The "big wheel and spokes" structure: the H1 headline is the wheel, H2/H3 subheadings are the spokes
- Cole's own words: "Formatting is the easiest 10x improvement you can make in your writing."

### 3.5 The Lean Writing content-expansion system

**Source**: [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary) + [Lean Writing Method](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-lean-writing-method-how-to-expand-short-form-content-into-longer-form-assets) (primary)

#### The expansion path
```
Tweet → Atomic Essay → Twitter Thread → LinkedIn Post → Blog/Ultimate Guide → Email Course → Digital Product → Online Course → Business
```

#### A three-step method
1. **Start Small** — test an idea publicly in its smallest form (a single tweet)
2. **Expand What Works** — once data confirms it works, expand it into a longer format
3. **Expand Again** — write the same successful topic repeatedly, from multiple angles, across multiple platforms

#### 10 angles for expansion (turning one piece into 10)

**Source**: [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)

Dickie Bush's suggestions: Tips, Stats, Steps, Lessons, Benefits, Reasons, Mistakes, Examples, Questions, Personal Stories

### 3.6 The Republishing Framework (cross-platform reuse)

**Source**: [Nicolas Cole Medium](https://nicolascole77.medium.com/the-ultimate-online-writing-republishing-framework-24028e37427d) (primary) + [Cole Tweet Thread](https://twunroll.com/article/1361689241978163202) (secondary archive)

1. Write an Atomic Essay
2. Post the image on Twitter
3. (Bonus) copy/paste the text as a thread
4. Find a relevant question on Quora and copy/paste it in, with the image
5. Copy/paste it on Medium
6. Copy/paste it on LinkedIn

Core insight: the same content reaches different audiences on different platforms — this isn't "reposting the same thing," it's "reaching different people"

### 3.8 The Hook-Story-Offer Framework

**Source**: [Ship 30 Hook-Story-Offer](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/the-hook-story-offer-framework-an-easy-copywriting-formula-for-beginners) (primary)

A four-step formula:
1. **Name The Benefit** — what the reader will get (mental clarity / financial freedom / etc.)
2. **Put The Benefit In An Unexpected Setting** — create a contrast (a weight-loss article -> set the scene in a Pizza Hut)
3. **Frame As Personal Story** — use "I..." / "How I..." to personalize the hook
4. **Edit For Clarity and Credibility** — make sure it's credible and clear

Example: "How I lost 10lbs in 4 weeks sitting in Pizza Hut"

## IV. Strategy: short tweets vs. long threads

### Sources
- [Lean Writing](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/lean-writing-on-twitter-how-to-turn-a-tweet-into-a-thread-into-an-atomic-essay) (primary)
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)

### Single Tweet (short tweets)
- Purpose: **validate an idea** — the smallest possible investment to test whether an opinion resonates
- Types: a statement of opinion, a quotable line, a one-sentence framework, a question
- Strategy: post several a day, watch which one performs best
- Cole calls it the "atomic unit" of a content system

### Atomic Essay (a 250-word mini-essay)
- Purpose: **go deep on one point** — more complete than a tweet, but doesn't require much investment
- Publishing format: screenshot posted to Twitter + a text-form thread
- Advantage: a structured exercise that forces you to make one point clearly within 250 words

### Twitter Thread (a long thread)
- Purpose: **demonstrate depth + drive large-scale growth** — the thread is Twitter's strongest growth engine
- Sweet spot: 5-7 tweets
- Timing: expand a short tweet/atomic essay into a thread once it's shown strong performance data
- Structure: must have a hook + a structured body + a TL;DR + a CTA

### Dickie Bush's content-mix strategy

**Source**: [Growth In Reverse](https://growthinreverse.com/dickie-bush/) (secondary analysis) + [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)

- **75% Reach Content (broad-appeal content)**: viral-friendly content on broad topics, spreadable regardless of who wrote it
- **25% Resonance Content (deep content)**: personal stories/unique perspective, builds the author's recognizability and trust
- Broad-appeal content acquires new followers, deep content retains them

## V. Category Design for writing positioning

### Sources
- [Snow Leopard Book Notes](https://www.danielscrivner.com/notes/snow-leopard-nicolas-cole) (detailed secondary notes)
- [Outliers Podcast #140](https://www.outlieracademy.com/episode/140) (primary podcast interview)

### The Content Pyramid
A five-tier evolution model:

| Tier | Name | Description |
|------|------|------|
| L1 | Consumption | passively consuming content |
| L2 | Curation | organizing and presenting other people's ideas (e.g. Tim Ferriss-style interviews) |
| L3 | Creation (Obvious) | original but linear/predictable content, connecting A to B |
| L4 | Creation (Non-Obvious) | cross-domain insight connecting unrelated fields (e.g. using esports experience to explain a writing technique) |
| L5 | Category Creation | creating a wholly new framework that redefines how an audience thinks |

### Disco Party vs Pool Party
Don't enter an existing market and say "I'm better" (a pool party) — instead create a wholly new option (a disco party). Let the audience choose between two different experiences, rather than comparing similar products against each other.

### The Tequila Test (a practical method)
1. List every piece of conventional advice on a topic
2. Cross all of it out
3. Write what's left
4. If you have nothing left after crossing it out — you don't yet have a genuinely differentiated point of view

### Languaging (the art of naming)
- Two words can change how a category is perceived: car -> electric car / romance -> military romance
- Cole's experience: he evolved from being paid by the word to realizing that "picking the right two words" alone can define a new category
- In practice: give your unique method/framework a name of its own (Ship 30 is itself an example of languaging)

### Views ≠ Success
Cole's own hard-earned lesson: accumulated 500 million reads but very low income -> after pivoting to a niche (digital writing), reads dropped but income exploded. **Quality engagement beats raw impressions.**

## VI. The seven-step method for writing an Atomic Essay

**Source**: [Atomic Essay Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-write-an-atomic-essay-a-beginners-guide) (primary)

1. **Pick a Specific Topic** — use guiding questions to overcome choice anxiety (write about content you're consuming / respond to someone / curate resources / teach a how-to / share a life lesson)
2. **Define Your Audience** — three audience buckets: General (relevant to everyone) / Niche (a specific group) / Industry (expert-level)
3. **Craft an Intriguing Headline** — must include 5 elements: who it's for/what it's about/emotion/promise/quantity
4. **Outline Key Points** — deliver exactly what the headline promises (if it says 7 ways, give 7 ways)
5. **Expand Main Points** — add personal stories, research data, the origin of the idea
6. **Edit for Readability** — capitalized headlines, bolded key words, strategic use of color and emoji, visual polish
7. **Publish & Track Data** — track the data after publishing, and let it guide what you write next

**Core principle**: "Your essay isn't about you. It's about your readers."

## VIII. Dickie Bush's Twitter growth strategy

### Sources
- [Bootstrapped Founder Podcast](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/dickie-bush-the-power-of-digital-writing/) (primary interview)
- [Nathan Barry Interview](https://nathanbarry.com/035-dickie-bush-100000-writing-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/building-a-6-figure-side-hustle-on-twitter/) (primary interview)
- [Podcast Notes](https://podcastnotes.org/uncategorized/dickie-bush-louis-shulman-kyle-bishop-writing/) (secondary compilation)

### Key strategies

**1. Twitter As Idea Refinery**
- Don't treat Twitter as a broadcast channel — treat it as an "idea refinery"
- Post a tweet -> watch the data -> expand what does well into a thread -> a thread that performs well becomes a newsletter -> eventually becomes a product

**2. Only-I-Can-Create Standard**
Before publishing, ask yourself: "would I want to consume this content myself?" "Would me, two years ago, have found this valuable?"

**3. Size of Question = Size of Audience**
- Big questions (how to be happier) = a big audience but shallow
- Small questions (how to grow a podcast from 2K to 10K) = a small audience but deep
- Strategy: use big questions to acquire, small questions to retain

**4. Bootstrap Initial Audience**
You don't need a big following. Just 2-3 people genuinely interested in your content is enough to start a feedback loop.

**5. Writing As Pre-Thinking**
"Writing is pre-thinking future conversations." Writing isn't expressing an idea you already have — it's thinking through writing.

**6. The inspiration behind his origin story**
Started in January 2020; after 9 months his newsletter had only 300 subscribers. He committed to writing a thread every day for 30 days straight. On day 28, Naval retweeted him, and his follower count doubled. Lesson: stick with it long enough for luck to have a chance to happen.

## IX. Common writing mistakes and fixes (Ship 30's teaching)

**Source**: [Ship 30 Ultimate Guide](https://www.ship30for30.com/post/how-to-start-writing-online-the-ship-30-for-30-ultimate-guide) (primary)

| Problem | Fix |
|------|------|
| Don't know what to write | the Endless Idea Generator (2-Year Test + 4A) |
| Perfectionism | pursue "junk" instead of perfection — publish first, iterate after |
| Procrastination | Sacred Hours — schedule writing into your calendar instead of finding spare time for it |
| Lack of confidence | writing itself is the process that builds confidence, not the other way around |
| Impostor syndrome | don't compete in an existing category — create your own |
| Not productive enough | rely on systems (frameworks), not inspiration, to drive output |
| Trouble picking a platform | don't start a personal blog — go where the traffic already is: Twitter/Medium/Quora, etc. |
| Can't find the time | make time, don't find time (Sacred Hours) |

## X. Business model and monetization paths

**Source**: [Snow Leopard Notes](https://www.danielscrivner.com/notes/snow-leopard-nicolas-cole) (detailed secondary notes) + [Passionfroot Interview](https://www.passionfroot.me/creators-on-air/digital-writing-twitter-growth-and-a-7-figure-side-hustle-with-dickie-bush) (primary interview)

### Cole's evolution path
- A ghostwriting agency ($2M in revenue, a 24-person team) -> discovered the ceiling on linearly scaling a services business
- Pivoted to: software (Typeshare) + education (Ship 30) + content products (books/newsletter)
- Core insight: **Demand Creation beats Demand Capture** — don't compete in an existing market, create new demand

### Bush's monetization matrix
- A community course (Ship 30 for 30)
- Ghostwriting training (the Premium Ghostwriting Academy)
- A paid newsletter
- Consulting/coaching

### The formula from writing to business
```
Daily Writing → Audience → Trust → Product → Revenue
     ↑                                        |
     └────── Data Feedback Loop ──────────────┘
```
