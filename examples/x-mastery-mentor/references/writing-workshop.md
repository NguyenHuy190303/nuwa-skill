## Short-tweet technique

**When to use**: validating an idea, everyday engagement, sharing an opinion

**Format choices**:
- A statement of opinion: one sharp stance ("Most people get X wrong...")
- A quotable line: one screenshot-worthy sentence
- A question: prompts replies (remember: a Reply is worth 27x a Like)
- A personal redefinition: "X isn't Y, X is Z"

**Dickie Bush's mix**: 75% broad-appeal content (viral-friendly, anyone could write it and it would spread) + 25% deep content (personal stories/unique perspective, builds recognizability)

## Hook-improvement examples (Before → After)

**Example 1: promoting an AI tool**
- Before: `I tested 5 AI coding tools. Here's what I found.`
- Problem: no curiosity gap, no credibility anchor, no concrete payoff
- After: `I mass-tested 5 AI coding tools on the same project (a full-stack app in 48 hours). One saved me 12 hours. The others were useless. A thread:`
- What changed: added a concrete scenario (full-stack app), a time anchor (48h), suspense (which one?), and credibility (real test data)

**Example 2: build in public**
- Before: `Just launched my new app. Check it out!`
- Problem: zero curiosity gap, talks at the reader, never answers "why should I care"
- After: `I built an iOS app with zero coding experience using only AI tools. It hit #1 Paid on the App Store in 3 days. Here's exactly how (and what almost killed it):`
- What changed: added an identity contrast (zero experience), a result anchor (#1 Paid), a time frame (3 days), suspense (almost killed it)

**Example 3: a contrarian take**
- Before: `AI coding tools are overrated.`
- Problem: too vague an opinion, no stake in the ground
- After: `Unpopular opinion: 90% of "AI coding tools" reviews on X are from people who never shipped a real product. I've shipped 3 apps. Here's what actually works vs what's just demo-ware:`
- What changed: added a concrete number (90%), credibility (shipped 3 apps), a foil (demo-ware), a delivery promise (what actually works)

## Thread technique

**Four-part structure** (Cole/Bush):

**1. Hook (the opening)**
Must answer: who is this for? What's it about? Why should I trust you? What will I get out of it?

Hook formula:
- A credibility element ("I studied 1,000...", "As a X-year Y...")
- A scenario anchor ("In 2024...", "Last week...")
- The core benefit ("How to...", "Why...")
- A concrete deliverable ("10 bullets on...", "A thread:")

**2. Main points (the body)**
- Each tweet's first sentence works as a mini-headline
- A 1/3/1 rhythm
- Each tweet can stand alone as its own piece
- Sweet spot: 8-12 tweets

**3. TL;DR**
- Write the TL;DR first, as an outline
- List only the headline points

**4. CTA (call to action)**
- Summarize the core takeaway
- State a clear next step (Follow/Bookmark/Newsletter)

**Cole's 7 templates**:
1. **Framework**: To solve X, I do Y, To achieve Z
2. **Story**: give the ending first -> then the beginning -> the reader reads through to find the "middle"
3. **Actionable**: a checklist of executable advice
4. **Curation**: "I read everything {person} has ever written — here are the best N pieces"
5. **Lessons**: lessons extracted from personal experience
6. **Mistakes**: "X mistakes I made doing Y"
7. **Contrarian**: challenges a common assumption

## Thread types unique to the AI niche

1. "I tested X, the results surprised me" — a real-world test of a new model/tool
2. "N lessons from $0 to $XK MRR" — a build-in-public recap
3. "This paper changed how I think" — a paper breakdown
4. "X vs Y: an in-depth comparison" — a head-to-head of tools/models
5. "I used AI to do X, saved N hours" — a hands-on case study

## Topic-selection system

**The Endless Idea Generator** (Cole/Bush — 100+ topics in 30 minutes):

**Step 1: the 2-year test**
Ask yourself: what problems did I solve in the last two years? What did I learn? List 3-5 topic buckets.

**Step 2: add constraints**
Add specific constraints to a topic until it's "uncomfortably specific." Your target reader = yourself, two years ago.

**Step 3: 4A × topic = a matrix**
One topic × 4 angles (Actionable/Analytical/Aspirational/Anthropological) × multiple formats = hundreds of pieces of content.

**Step 4: pick the 3 that excite you most, and start writing**

**Bush's big-question/small-question strategy**:
- Big questions (how to be happier) = big audience but shallow -> for acquiring new followers
- Small questions (how to grow a podcast from 2K to 10K) = small audience but deep -> for retaining them
