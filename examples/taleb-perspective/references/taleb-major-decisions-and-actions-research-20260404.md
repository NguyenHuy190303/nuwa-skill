# Taleb (Nassim Nicholas Taleb) - Decision Record and Actions Research

> Research date: 2026-04-04
> Research goal: map Taleb's key decisions and actual actions across his career, and analyze the consistency between his words and his deeds

---

## I. Key decisions in his trading career

### 1. "Black Monday" 1987 — the day that made his reputation

**[Event]** In 1986, 28-year-old Taleb, working at CS First Boston, built a large position of deep out-of-the-money Eurodollar futures call options, along with deep out-of-the-money foreign-exchange puts — many priced more than 20 standard deviations out.

**[Decision]** Buy extremely cheap options that "will almost certainly lose money," bleeding small losses steadily while waiting for an extreme event.

**[Inferred decision logic]** Markets systematically underprice extreme events. Most of the time these options expire worthless, but once a Black Swan appears, the payoff is explosive. This became the prototype for his later "barbell strategy."

**[Result]** On October 19, 1987, the Dow crashed 22.6%; the Fed's emergency liquidity injection caused Eurodollar futures to spike. The bank made $35-40 million on Taleb's position. Taleb himself has said 97% of all the money he ever made in his life came from that single day, Black Monday.

**Consistency between words and actions: complete.** The core logic of all his later theories (Black Swan, antifragility, the barbell strategy) had already been put into practice with real money at age 28. The theory grew out of the practice, not the other way around.

---

### 2. Closing Empirica and exiting trading (2004-2005)

**[Event]** Taleb founded the hedge fund Empirica Capital, focused on tail-risk strategies. In the years after 2000, market volatility stayed persistently low, and the fund suffered continuous losses — large losses in 2001-2002, and under 10% returns in 2003-2004.

**[Decision]** Closed Empirica in 2004, exited trading entirely in 2005, and transitioned into a full-time writer and academic.

**[Inferred decision logic]** A tail-risk strategy will keep "bleeding" in a low-volatility environment — this is inherent to the strategy's design: small losses most of the time, big wins in extreme moments. But sustained losses take a huge psychological toll on investor trust. Taleb chose to exit trading and pivot to writing and academia, spreading the wisdom of "how to benefit from extreme events" in book form instead.

**[Result]** Three years after he exited trading, the 2008 financial crisis hit. By then he was no longer directly managing a fund, but his student/protégé Mark Spitznagel's Universa fund put his theory into flawless practice, making huge gains in the 2008 crisis. Taleb himself became famous through *The Black Swan*.

**Consistency between words and actions: an interesting tension.** In hindsight, the timing of his exit looks "too early" — holding on for three more years would have let him catch 2008 himself. But from another angle, his exit precisely demonstrates that he understood "the cost of waiting for a Black Swan" — the pain of continuous losses was real. He hasn't used hindsight to flatter his own exit.

---

### 3. The Universa fund and the COVID crash (March 2020)

**[Event]** Universa Investments is managed by Mark Spitznagel, with Taleb serving as "distinguished scientific advisor." In March 2020 global markets crashed due to COVID.

**[Decision]** Taleb, as advisor, continued to provide theoretical support for Universa's tail-risk strategy (without directly managing the investments).

**[Inferred decision logic]** Maintaining "skin in the game" — he doesn't just write theory, he ties himself to an actual trading strategy through his advisory role.

**[Result]** Universa returned 3,612% in the single month of March 2020, and 4,144% from the start of the year through the end of March. A portfolio of 96.7% S&P 500 + 3.3% Universa suffered almost no loss in March 2020, and has returned 11.5% annualized since 2008 (versus 7.9% for the S&P 500).

**A key episode — CalPERS's costly mistake**: the California Public Employees' Retirement System (CalPERS) hired Universa in 2017 to hedge tail risk, but incoming chief investment officer Ben Meng terminated the program in October 2019 to "cut costs." Five months later COVID crashed the markets, and CalPERS missed out on over $1 billion in hedging gains as a result. Taleb publicly attacked Ben Meng on Twitter, calling his pushback against tail-risk hedging "extremely sloppy."

**Consistency between words and actions: highly consistent.** His theory predicted the value of tail risk, and Universa's actual performance validated the theory. The CalPERS case is a perfect cautionary tale — exactly the behavior pattern Taleb has repeatedly criticized: "thinking insurance is too expensive beforehand, regretting it bitterly afterward."

## II. His COVID response — a theorist validated in real time

**[Event]** On January 25, 2020, with global confirmed cases under 2,000 and deaths under 60, Taleb, together with systems scientist Joe Norman, published an academic paper warning that COVID's spread would be "nonlinear."

**[Decision]** While nearly everyone else believed it was "just another flu," he publicly called for countries to take extreme measures immediately — "kill it before it incubates." He argued that all flights should have been grounded in January, and that subsidizing the airlines' losses would have cost far less than the trillions the world later lost.

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is a direct application of his Precautionary Principle: for a threat with systemic risk and nonlinear spread characteristics, even if the probability looks low, extreme preventive measures should be taken, because if it does happen, the losses are unbearable.

**[Result]** He turned out to be completely right. He also repeatedly corrected the media: COVID wasn't a Black Swan, it was a "White Swan" — it was foreseeable, with ample warning signals, and was simply ignored by incompetent decision-makers.

**Consistency between words and actions: extremely consistent.** This wasn't hindsight — the paper was published on January 26, 2020, 45 days before the WHO declared a global pandemic. He applied his own theoretical framework (fat-tailed distributions, the precautionary principle, systemic risk) directly to a real-time decision, and left a timestamped record of it.

## III. A shift in position — the 180-degree turn on Bitcoin

**[Event]** In 2018, Taleb wrote the foreword for Saifedean Ammous's *The Bitcoin Standard*, calling Bitcoin "the first organic currency" and "insurance against a currency controlled by government." During the 2019 Lebanese banking crisis, he further praised Bitcoin for existing outside the central-bank system.

**[Decision]** Starting in March 2021 he sold off all his crypto holdings, and in June of the same year published the paper "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility," declaring Bitcoin's value to be "exactly zero."

**[Inferred decision logic]** His stated reasons: (1) Bitcoin requires continuous participant interest to sustain its value and has no intrinsic value; (2) its volatility is too high for it to function as currency; (3) the crypto community has "turned into a cult." A deeper reason may be that he originally saw Bitcoin's "antifragile" properties (decentralization, censorship resistance), but later came to recognize that in actual operation Bitcoin isn't antifragile at all — it's highly dependent on electricity, network infrastructure, and speculators' confidence.

**[Result]** He became one of the least popular figures in the crypto community. Bitcoin supporters accused him of a criticism that violates his own principles — someone who advocates "decentralization" and "distrust of government" turned against the largest decentralized-currency experiment.

**Consistency between words and actions: contested.** Critics see this as a textbook case of saying one thing and doing another. But from within Taleb's own framework, he would argue: (1) changing your mind isn't itself the problem — clinging to a wrong view is; (2) his core principle is "antifragility," not "decentralization" — once he judged Bitcoin lacked antifragility, he should abandon it. Still, only three years passed between writing the foreword and publishing the paper attacking it, and the speed of the reversal does raise doubts about whether his original judgment was sufficiently careful.

## IV. The GMO controversy — a radical application of the precautionary principle

**[Event]** Taleb, along with Bar-Yam and others, published a paper arguing that genetically modified organisms (GMOs) should be subject to the "precautionary principle," because they could pose a systemic risk of "global-scale ecological ruin."

**[Decision]** Publicly opposed GMOs and published academic papers, while also refusing to take part in public debate with critics (backing out of a debate with David Ropeik), and using insulting language against critics on social media (calling them "idiots" or "paid shills").

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is an extension of his precautionary principle — for a technology that could cause irreversible global-scale disaster, extreme caution is warranted even if the scientific mainstream considers it safe. He believes scientists underestimate the "unknown unknowns."

**[Result]** Mainstream geneticists and science journalists broadly considered his argument seriously flawed — he conflated the essential difference between traditional breeding and genetic modification, and his "ecological ruin" risk calculation lacked an empirical basis. More critically, his refusal to debate and his personal attacks violated the very principle he emphasizes in *Skin in the Game* — that intellectuals should bear the consequences of their own views.

**Consistency between words and actions: partly inconsistent.** Refusing to debate is a clear contradiction — someone who advocates "skin in the game" chose to run from a challenge. His argument may be worth discussing, but how he handled the controversy (personal attacks plus refusing to debate) stands in ironic contrast to the standard he uses to criticize "intellectuals with no skin in the game."

## V. His personal-life practices — putting his words into physical action

### 5a. Weightlifting/deadlifts

**[Event]** Taleb took up weight training in middle age, focused on the deadlift.

**[Decision]** Chose the deadlift as his core training exercise, doing only 1-2 maximal-effort attempts per session, chasing new personal records rather than doing multiple repetitive sets. He can deadlift 330 pounds (about 150 kg).

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is a direct physical application of "antifragility" and the "barbell strategy" — brief, extreme stress stimulates the body to expect bigger challenges and grow stronger as a result. He also wrote the foreword to Mark Rippetoe's *Starting Strength*, theorizing strength training as "learning from tail events."

**Consistency between words and actions: highly consistent.** This isn't just talk — he regularly shares training videos and numbers on social media. He's even said, "I do my Twitter fights at the gym, replying between sets." His training method fully matches his philosophy: not gentle day-to-day repetition, but intermittent extreme challenge.

### 5b. Diet and fasting

**[Event]** Taleb practices intermittent fasting, following a diet similar to paleo/Mediterranean eating.

**[Decision]** Eats only lunch and dinner, 17 hours apart. Eats only "food that has existed in the Levant for a very long time." Drinks only "beverages that have existed for over a thousand years." As a Greek Orthodox Christian, he observes over 200 fasting days a year.

**[Inferred decision logic]** A physical practice of antifragile philosophy — creating stress through intermittent hunger to keep the body adaptive. Using the Lindy Effect to filter food — the longer a food or drink has existed, the more likely it is to be harmless to the human body.

**Consistency between words and actions: highly consistent.** He practices his theory with his own body, rather than just talking about health in papers the way he criticizes "IYIs" (credentialed but wisdom-less intellectuals) for doing.

### 5c. Religious practice

**[Event]** Taleb comes from a Lebanese Greek Orthodox family that was once prominent in Lebanese politics (his grandfather and great-grandfather were both deputy prime ministers, his grandfather a supreme-court judge), but the family lost its political standing and wealth in the 1975 civil war.

**[Decision]** Although he isn't, intellectually, a "believer" in the traditional sense, he strictly observes every ritual of the Orthodox calendar, including 200+ fasting days. He believes religion has little to do with "believing in a supernatural god," and is more about culture, community, and ritual.

**[Inferred decision logic]** This is again an application of the Lindy Effect — religious rituals that have persisted for two thousand years must contain time-tested wisdom, even if you can't fully explain it rationally. He observes the rituals not because he believes the literal doctrine, but because he trusts the filtering power of time.

**Consistency between words and actions: deeply consistent.** This is where Taleb's intellectual coherence shows most clearly — he isn't a rationalist atheist (even though he criticizes many rationalists), nor a blind religious believer. He reinterprets and practices religion through his own framework (antifragility, the Lindy Effect, skin in the game).

## VI. His social-media behavior — his pattern under pressure

**[Event]** Taleb is extremely combative on Twitter, and has had fierce disputes with countless people, including hedge-fund manager Cliff Asness, psychologist Steven Pinker, and anthropologist David Graeber.

**[Behavior pattern]**
- Uses insulting language against critics ("idiot," "moron," etc.)
- Blocks large numbers of people who disagree with him, especially science journalists
- Accuses critics of being "paid off" or "lacking skin in the game"
- Backed out of an already-scheduled public debate on GMOs
- Repeatedly mischaracterized Steven Pinker's views in his criticism of him

**[Inferred behavioral logic]** Possible explanations: (1) he genuinely believes most critics aren't worth engaging with intellectually; (2) combativeness is a strategy — extreme phrasing amplifies the signal and draws attention; (3) coming from a Lebanese elite family's cultural background, there may be an ingrained disdain for arguing with people he considers beneath him; (4) he's admitted to replying to Twitter arguments at the gym, suggesting these fights are more of a pastime for him than serious intellectual debate.

**Consistency between words and actions: clearly inconsistent.** This is Taleb's biggest contradiction:

1. He advocates "skin in the game" — but blocking critics means he's unwilling to bear the discomfort of being challenged
2. He criticizes "intellectuals with no skin in the game" — but backing out of the GMO debate is itself dodging intellectual accountability
3. He respects "things that have withstood the test of time" — but a Twitter feud is precisely the shortest-sighted, most emotional form of intellectual expression
4. He criticizes "IYIs" for using authority to browbeat people — but he himself frequently uses identity-based attacks in arguments

## VII. Composite assessment: a scorecard for consistency between words and actions

| Dimension | Consistency score | Notes |
|------|-----------|------|
| Trading strategy vs. theory | ★★★★★ | The theory came entirely out of practice; Universa validated the framework |
| COVID response | ★★★★★ | The January 26 paper is a real-time, timestamped validation |
| Physical training/diet | ★★★★☆ | Practices theory with his own body; the deadlifts and fasting are both genuinely real |
| Religious practice | ★★★★☆ | Reinterprets and practices religious tradition through his own framework |
| Bitcoin stance | ★★★☆☆ | Changing his mind isn't itself wrong, but the 180-degree reversal within 3 years was very fast |
| The GMO controversy | ★★☆☆☆ | His argument is debatable, but refusing to debate plus personal attacks contradicts "skin in the game" |
| Social-media behavior | ★★☆☆☆ | Combative, blocks people, insults people — no different from the "arrogant intellectual" he criticizes |

### Overall judgment

Taleb is **highly consistent between words and actions on the big things** — his trading strategy, risk theory, COVID warning, and physical training have all been validated by real action backing his beliefs. His core belief system (Black Swan, antifragility, skin in the game, the precautionary principle) has a rare internal coherence, and all of it is backed by practice.

But in **interpersonal interaction and intellectual argument**, he displays a clear double standard — demanding "skin in the game" from others, while choosing to dodge (backing out of debates) or attack (personal insults) when challenged himself. His social-media behavior pattern stands in sharp contrast to the image of the "wise ancient merchant" he portrays in his books.

One possible explanation: at the "systems level" (strategy, theory, lifestyle), Taleb is a disciplined practitioner, but at the "personal level" (ego, social interaction, argument), he has blind spots like everyone else. In his own words, this may just be "domain dependence" — the same person can be highly rational in one domain and far less so in another.
