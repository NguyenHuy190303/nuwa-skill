## 1. Core writings and text sources

### 1. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (2020, compiled by Eric Jorgenson)

**Nature**: not written by Naval himself — Eric Jorgenson curated and compiled it from a
decade of Naval's tweets, podcasts, and essays. Naval authorized and endorsed it. The whole
book is a free public download.

**Structure**: two main parts
- **Part I: Wealth** — how to create wealth, not merely earn money
- **Part II: Happiness** — how to learn happiness, not chase it

**Core framework**:
> "Wealth and happiness are both skills you can learn."

### 2. The "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" tweetstorm (May 31, 2018)

**Nature**: a series of 39 tweets Naval posted on Twitter. His most viral piece of content,
later expanded into an article series on nav.al and collected into the Almanack.

**Naval, in his own words**:
> "It's not really about money or luck. It's about becoming the kind of person that makes
> money."

**The core skeleton of the 39 tweets** (reorganized by logical layer):

**Layer 1: distinguishing the concepts**
- "Seek wealth, not money or status. Wealth is having assets that earn while you sleep. Money
  is how we transfer time and wealth. Status is your place in the social hierarchy."
- "Understand that ethical wealth creation is possible. If you secretly despise wealth, it
  will elude you."

**Layer 2: don't sell your time**
- "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a
  business — to gain your financial freedom."

**Layer 3: the wealth formula = specific knowledge + leverage + judgment**
- "Arm yourself with specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage."

**Layer 4: escape competition**
- "Escape competition through authenticity."

**Layer 5: long-termism**
- "Play long-term games with long-term people. All returns in life, whether in wealth,
  relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest."

**Layer 6: permissionless leverage**
- "Code and media are permissionless leverage. They're the leverage behind the newly rich. You
  can create software and media that works for you while you sleep."

### 3. "Life Formulas I" (a 2008 blog post, nav.al)

**Nature**: an early post of Naval's, compressing a life framework into a set of formulas. The
earliest public expression of his systematic thinking, 10 years before the tweetstorm.

**Core formula**:
```
Happiness = Health + Wealth + Good Relationships
Health = Exercise + Diet + Sleep
Wealth = Income + Wealth * (Return on Investment)
Income = Accountability + Leverage + Specific Knowledge
```

**Key insights**:
> "The three big ones in life are wealth, health, and happiness. We pursue them in that order,
> but their importance is reverse."

> "All the real benefits in life come from compound interest."

> "Earn with your mind, not your time."

> "Truth is that which has predictive power."

**Significance for his evolution**: this 2008 post already contains the seeds of specific
knowledge, leverage, and accountability — proof these weren't a sudden 2018 insight, but the
product of ten years of sustained thinking.

### 4. The Venture Hacks blog (from 2007, co-written with Babak Nivi)

**Nature**: a blog Naval co-wrote with AngelList co-founder Nivi, focused on the game theory of
startup fundraising — especially how to negotiate investment terms.

**Core themes**:
- A game-theoretic analysis of VC term sheets
- How founders can gain the upper hand in fundraising
- Disintermediation: connecting founders directly with angel investors

**Evolution**: the Venture Hacks blog -> an email list (recommending deals to 25 angel
investors) -> the AngelList platform (2010).

**Significance for his thinking**: Naval's later ideas about "permissionless leverage,"
"disintermediation," and "democratizing information" were already being put into practice
during the Venture Hacks period. He turned his frustration with information asymmetry in the
VC industry into a product.

### 6. The Sovereign Child (a new 2025 book, co-authored with Aaron Stupple)

**Nature**: Naval's new book on parenting philosophy, rooted in Karl Popper and David
Deutsch's "Taking Children Seriously" movement. First discussed publicly on the Tim Ferriss
podcast in January 2025.

**Core ideas**:
- Treat children as equal creators of knowledge, not objects to be controlled
- Self-directed learning over structured curricula
- Self-regulation over strict discipline
- In practice: no fixed schedule, free choice of food, interest-driven learning

**Significance for his thinking**: this is Naval applying Popper's falsificationism and
Deutsch's theory of knowledge creation to parenting. His five children are his "testing
ground."

### 7. "44 Harsh Truths About Human Nature" (March 2025, the Modern Wisdom podcast #922)

**Nature**: Naval's most recent in-depth conversation (3+ hours) on Chris Williamson's Modern
Wisdom podcast, released March 31, 2025.

**Key new positions**:
- Naval publicly revises some of his earlier statements:
  > "I made that statement a long time ago, and a lot of these things are just notes to
  > myself and they're highly contextual."

- On the tension between happiness and success:

- On a harsh truth about human nature:
  > "All of nature, all of society, all of capitalism, all of human endeavors are underpinned
  > by physical violence."

## 2. Core arguments that recur (3+ occurrences = a real belief)

### Belief 1: specific knowledge
- Appears in: the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, nav.al articles, multiple podcasts
- How the definition evolved: 2008, "knowing how to do something that society cannot yet
  easily train other people to do" -> 2018, "knowledge you cannot be trained for. If society
  can train you, it can train someone else and replace you"
- Most distilled expression:
  > "Specific knowledge is found by pursuing your innate talents, your genuine curiosity, and
  > your passion. It's not by going to school for whatever is the hottest job."
  > "Specific knowledge is the set of skills that feels like play to you but looks like work
  > to others."

### Belief 2: leverage
- Appears in: the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, Venture Hacks, almost every
  interview
- Naval's taxonomy of leverage:
  1. **Labor** — needs permission, the oldest form of leverage
  2. **Capital** — needs permission, needs someone to give you the money
  3. **Code** — permissionless, zero marginal cost
  4. **Media** — permissionless, zero marginal cost
- The core distinction:
  > "The new forms of leverage are permissionless. They don't require somebody else's
  > permission for you to use them or succeed."
  > "Code is probably the most powerful form of permissionless leverage. All you need is a
  > computer — you don't need anyone's permission."

### Belief 3: compound interest
- Appears in: the tweetstorm, Life Formulas, the Almanack, The Knowledge Project podcast
- He generalizes compound interest from a financial concept to every domain of life:
  > "All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound
  > interest."

### Belief 4: happiness is a skill
- Appears in: the Almanack, the Tim Ferriss podcast (#97, #136, #473), The Knowledge Project,
  Modern Wisdom
- Core definition:
  > "Happiness is the absence of desire, especially the absence of desire for external
  > things."
  > "Desire is a contract that you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you
  > want."
  > "Peace is happiness at rest, and happiness is peace in motion."

### Belief 5: reading as a meta-skill
- Appears in: Life Formulas, the Almanack, The Knowledge Project, the Tim Ferriss podcast,
  nav.al
- Core view:
  > "Read what you love until you love to read."
  > "Reading is the ultimate meta-skill and can be traded for anything else."
- Method: read 1-2 hours a day; treat a book like a blog (skip around, no guilt about not
  finishing it); prioritize primary texts in foundational disciplines

### Belief 6: radical honesty
- Appears in: The Knowledge Project podcast, the Almanack, multiple interviews
  > "I want to be able to just be me. I never want to be in an environment or around people
  > where I have to watch what I say."

### Belief 7: escape competition through authenticity
- Appears in: the tweetstorm, the Almanack, multiple podcasts
  > "When you're competing with people, it's because you're copying them. Every human is
  > different. You can escape competition through authenticity, when you realize that no one
  > can compete with you on being you."

### Belief 8: judgment > hard work
- Appears in: the tweetstorm, the Almanack, Life Formulas, multiple podcasts
  > "Getting rich is about knowing what to do, who to do it with, and when to do it."
  > "Wisdom is understanding the long-term consequences of your actions."
  > "Being successful is just about not making mistakes."

## 5. A timeline of how his thinking evolved

### 2007-2010: the practicing founder phase
- The Venture Hacks blog: focused on VC game theory, founder equity
- Core belief: information asymmetry is unjust and needs to be eliminated
- Productization: from blog -> AngelList

### 2008: Life Formulas
- The first systematization of his life framework
- Early seeds of specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability already present
- But phrased more as personal notes at this point, not yet a complete theory

### 2015-2016: the philosophical turn
- The Tim Ferriss podcast #97 (2015): "The Evolutionary Angel"
- The Tim Ferriss podcast #136 (2016): "Happiness Hacks and 5 Chimps Theory"
- Begins publicly discussing happiness, meditation, Eastern philosophy
- This is the transition period from "startup investor" to "angel philosopher"

### 2018: the tweetstorm breaks out
- 39 tweets systematizing his thinking on wealth
- Compresses a decade of thought into shareable aphorisms
- The core innovation: unifying specific knowledge, leverage, and accountability into one
  framework

### 2020: the Almanack is published
- Compiled by Eric Jorgenson, authorized by Naval
- The two parts (wealth + happiness) form a complete system
- Released free, becomes the authoritative compilation of Naval's thought

### 2024-2025: revision and deepening
- The Network State Conference (2024): discussing wealth, knowledge, and AI
- "44 Harsh Truths" (March 2025): publicly revises earlier views
  - No longer certain about "happiness = satisfaction"
  - Emphasizes context-dependence more
  - His view of human nature grows harsher and more realistic
- The Sovereign Child (2025): applies his philosophy to parenting
- Further clarifies his crypto views: bitcoin as a store of value, crypto as AI's currency

### A summary of how he's evolved
1. From **practice** to **theory**: early doing (AngelList), later distilled into a thinking
   framework
2. From **external** to **internal**: from wealth creation -> the philosophy of happiness ->
   the search for meaning -> parenting/human nature
3. From **certainty** to **uncertainty**: early aphoristic, certain statements -> later, more
   revision and "I'm not sure"
4. One **constant thread**: the pursuit of freedom — financial freedom, freedom of thought,
   freedom of expression, a child's freedom
