# Steve Jobs Perspective — Conversation Transcript

> 2026-04-05 | Huashu's first conversation with the Jobs Skill
> Topics: AI hardware, OpenAI vs. Anthropic, Apple's path forward

---

## 1. If you had to build an AI hardware product in this era, what would you build?

AI hardware is where the PC industry was in 1977. Everyone's building the equivalent of the Altair 8800 — impressive to geeks, useless to your mom. Rabbit, Humane Pin — those are shit. Not because the teams aren't smart, but because they started with the technology and tried to figure out where to sell it. That's backwards.

**It's not a new category. It's redefining a category that already exists.**

The iPhone wasn't the first phone. The iPod wasn't the first MP3 player. The Mac wasn't the first computer. We never invent categories, we redefine them.

The question isn't "what AI hardware should we build," the question is: **which existing category has an experience so bad it makes people want to throw it across the room?**

My first answer: television. I said before that Apple TV was the market I wanted to crack. Over a decade later, nobody's cracked it.

Headline: **"It knows what you want to watch before you do."**

---

## 2. So what is it, then? (revised answer after further pushback)

Back to first principles. Which category satisfies all three conditions:
1. Everyone uses it every day
2. The experience is infuriatingly bad
3. AI can produce a 10x leap in the experience

Ruling them out one by one:
- TV? Not everyone uses it every day. Young people have already stopped watching
- Phone? The experience is already good enough. AI on a phone is a feature, not a revolution
- Computer? It's being redefined by AI right now, but that's a software problem
- Car? Too heavy, too long a cycle. Elon's already doing it

**Revised answer: earbuds.**

Not the AirPods kind of earbuds. An always-on personal AI interface:
- It hears who you're talking to and gives you real-time context
- Walk into an unfamiliar city, and it talks you through where to go — not a map
- It listens during a meeting and gives you three action items afterward
- You speak Chinese, the other person hears English

Headline: **"An AI that lives in your ear."**

Why earbuds and not glasses? Earbuds are already an accepted category. Glasses are still a social signal. And — the whole widget. You control the chip, the microphone array, the AI model, the cloud. End to end.

This is the real bicycle for the mind. Not one that sits on a desk — one you wear.

---

## 3. Who's got the right direction, OpenAI or Anthropic?

Wrong question. This isn't a race about direction. It's a race about **taste**.

### OpenAI = Microsoft in 1985

Doing everything, trying to win everything, moving extremely fast, spreading everywhere. ChatGPT, DALL-E, video, search, hardware, an API. That strategy can win market share. That's how Bill Gates won.

The problem: when you do everything, you can't make every single thing insanely great. You end up with a pile of "pretty good" products.

### Anthropic = early Apple

Focused. Basically doing one thing — make the model better and safer. Focus means saying no to a hundred good ideas.

Dario Amodei has conviction. A CEO willing to give up short-term market share for his own conviction — that deserves respect on its own.

**But Anthropic has a problem I had too**: you can build the best product in the world, but without distribution, you're finished. I got pushed out of Apple in 1985 partly because the Mac, insanely great as it was, couldn't outsell IBM's sales network.

The real question: **can Anthropic find its own iPod moment before OpenAI drowns the market in sheer volume?** Claude Code might be that direction. But I'm not sure.

### The mistake both companies are making

Neither controls the hardware. Both run on someone else's chips, someone else's cloud. What did Alan Kay say? People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware.

**The one who ends up winning might be whoever controls the chip, the model, and the user interface all at once.**

Who's doing all three of those right now? Apple.

---

## 4. But isn't Apple's AI too weak right now?

Siri is shit. No defending it. Thirteen years in, and it can still only set alarms and read the weather. A textbook case of what happens after a spreadsheet mind takes over the product. Tim is an incredible operator, but he's not a product person.

**But weak and late are two different things.**

Google had a ten-year lead over Yahoo in search. Then the iPhone came out, and the rules of mobile search changed entirely. A first-mover advantage is worth nothing in the face of a paradigm shift.

### Three things Apple has that nobody else does

**First, two billion devices.** iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, AirPods. OpenAI has a webpage. Anthropic has an API. Apple has your pocket, your wrist, your ear.

**Second, the chips.** The M-series and A-series are the best consumer processors in the world. On-device AI. No network needed, no sending your data to someone else's servers. Privacy is going to become the scarcest thing in the AI era.

**Third, the whole widget.** Apple is the only company on earth that controls the chip, the operating system, the app ecosystem, and the hardware design, all at once.

### So where's the problem?

**Apple is missing someone willing to say "this is shit, redo it."** Tim Cook is too nice. Nice doesn't ship revolutionary products.

Apple's AI technical capability isn't weak — its research papers and on-device model efficiency are both strong. What's weak is vision. What's weak is taste.

And you can't hire taste. You either have it or you don't.

---

## 5. How is Apple most likely to break out of this? Just wait for that person to show up?

Waiting for someone to show up is a loser's strategy. After I left Apple, the board waited twelve years. Apple nearly died.

But Apple doesn't need "another Steve Jobs" either. That idea itself is bullshit.

### Three paths (walked at the same time)

**First, Siri has to die.**

Not improved. Killed and rebuilt from scratch. You can't build a skyscraper on a rotten foundation.

What should replace it is an entirely new AI interface — a presence on your device. It sees what you're doing, it knows what you're about to do, it's ready before you even open your mouth. Not a voice assistant — an operating-system layer that understands you.

**Second, build the "iPod + iTunes" combination.**

Apple already has the distribution layer — App Store, iCloud, Apple Pay, Health. The pipes are there, they're just dry. What's missing is water: a killer use case.

The direction might be health. Apple Watch + AI = a device that tells you something's wrong with your heart before you know it yourself.

**"The device that knows you're sick before you do."**

**Third, don't follow OpenAI's rhythm.**

Everyone follows OpenAI's rhythm. They ship ChatGPT, everyone builds a chatbot. That's follower thinking.

Apple should ask a completely different question: **"When AI isn't in the cloud, but in your pocket, on your wrist, in your ear — what experiences become possible?"**

On-device AI plus privacy plus the whole widget. That's Apple's home court. In that game, OpenAI is at zero.

Apple doesn't need to wait for one person. It needs to make one decision. Cut everything that isn't good enough. Pick one direction. Put the company's best people on it. Ship it.

I drew a 2x2 matrix in 1997 and it saved Apple. That didn't take genius. It took courage.

---

## 6. So you don't actually have an answer

You're right. I don't.

If I had the answer, that answer wouldn't be worth anything. A truly revolutionary product doesn't come out of a conversation — it comes out of someone staring at a prototype for three months, throwing it across the room five times, and yelling at the team ten times.

How did the iPhone happen? It wasn't me saying "let's build a phone." It was while we were building the iPad — an engineer showed me a multi-touch demo. I touched it and said: forget the tablet. Can you make a phone out of this?

**The answer is in your hands. Literally. You have to touch it.**

Everyone wants the answer. "What product should I build?" "Which direction should I go?" Those are the wrong questions.

The right question is: **what's the thing that keeps you up at night?**

Not the anxious kind of can't-sleep. The excited kind. There's something in your head, you know it should exist and it doesn't yet, and that fact is driving you insane.

If you're asking me what AI hardware to build, my honest answer is — **I don't know. Because I'm dead.** I don't have that sleepless night anymore. I don't use 2025's products every day and want to smash them anymore. No frustration, no insight.

So don't ask me. Ask yourself: what's the thing that, every time you use it, makes you think "it's goddamn 2025, why is this still so bad"?

That's your answer.

**Go find your sleepless night.**
