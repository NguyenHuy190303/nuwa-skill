# Steve Jobs Full Life Timeline (1955-2011)

> Research date: 2026-04-05
> Sources: the English Wikipedia, Britannica, CNN, NPR, ABC News, Biography.com, Macworld, Fast Company, the Library of Congress, and others

---

## Chapter One: Early years (1955-1976)

### Birth and adoption

- **February 24, 1955**: Steven Paul Jobs was born in San Francisco, California. His biological father, Abdulfattah "John" Jandali (a Syrian-born political-science graduate student), and biological mother, Joanne Schieble (a University of Wisconsin graduate student), were unmarried; Schieble's father opposed her marrying an Arab, so the child was put up for adoption
- **1955**: adopted by Paul Jobs (a machinist/used-car dealer) and Clara Jobs (an accountant), settling in Mountain View, California
- Jobs later learned he was adopted, which became an important part of his psychological makeup for the rest of his life. Paul Jobs assured him: "you were specially chosen"

### Upbringing

- Grew up in the heart of Silicon Valley — Mountain View and Los Altos — surrounded by neighbors who were mostly Hewlett-Packard engineers
- Paul Jobs's garage became Steve's first "laboratory," where his father taught him to take apart and rebuild electronics and car parts
- **His obsession with craftsmanship** came from his adoptive father: Paul Jobs insisted the back of a piece of refinished furniture be made just as perfect as the front — "even though no one will ever see it"

### School and early interests

- **1968 (age 13)**: called HP CEO Bill Hewlett to ask for spare parts — not only got the parts, but also landed a summer internship
- **around 1969-1970**: met Steve Wozniak at Homestead High School (Woz was 18, Jobs was 13); the two became friends over a shared passion for electronics
- **1971**: worked with Wozniak to build a "Blue Box," an illegal device that could place free long-distance calls. Jobs handled sales, Woz handled the technology — their first joint commercial venture
- **1972**: graduated from Homestead High School

### Reed College and spiritual exploration

- **Fall 1972**: enrolled at Reed College in Portland, Oregon
- **late 1972/early 1973**: dropped out after one semester — he felt it was wasteful for his parents to spend their savings on an education when he didn't know what he wanted to do with it. But he kept auditing classes on campus for another 18 months
- **Auditing the calligraphy class**: Professor Robert Palladino's class sparked a deep interest in typography and design, which directly shaped the Macintosh's fonts a decade later. This is the central example of his later "connecting the dots" philosophy
- **1974**: returned to California and worked as a technician at Atari. That same year he and his close friend Daniel Kottke took a 7-month spiritual journey to India, where he was introduced to Zen Buddhism
- **1974-1975**: began practicing at the Los Altos Zen Center under teacher Kobun Chino Otogawa. Zen would leave a lifelong mark on his product philosophy: simplicity, focus, intuition

### Reuniting with Wozniak

- **1975**: Wozniak started attending the Homebrew Computer Club and began designing a personal computer
- **late 1975-early 1976**: Jobs saw the commercial potential in the circuit board Wozniak had built by hand — Woz just wanted to share the design for free, but Jobs convinced him to start a company together

## Chapter Two: Founding Apple (1976-1985)

### Starting in a garage

- **April 1, 1976**: Steve Jobs (21), Steve Wozniak (25), and Ronald Wayne (41) formally founded Apple Computer, Inc. Wayne held a 10% stake but sold it back for $800 just 12 days later
- Initial funding: Jobs sold his VW van ($1,500), Wozniak sold his HP-65 calculator ($500)
- **The Apple I**: a hand-assembled bare circuit board priced at $666.66, sold mainly through the local computer store The Byte Shop, with about 200 units produced

### The Apple II and commercial liftoff

- **January 1977**: Apple Computer was formally incorporated
- **April 1977**: the Apple II launched at the West Coast Computer Faire — the first personal computer built for mass consumers, with color display, a keyboard, and a plastic case
- **1977**: Mike Markkula joined, investing $250,000 and bringing business experience, becoming Apple's third key figure
- **May 17, 1978**: daughter Lisa Brennan-Jobs was born to Chrisann Brennan. Jobs denied paternity, even claiming in court filings that he was "sterile"
- **1979**: visited Xerox PARC (the Palo Alto Research Center) and saw a demo of the graphical user interface (GUI) and the mouse, which left a deep impression on him. This visit directly gave rise to the Lisa and Macintosh projects
- **December 12, 1980**: Apple went public at $22 a share, closing its first day at $29, a market cap of $1.78 billion. At just 25, Jobs was worth over $250 million
- **1980**: a DNA test proved Lisa was Jobs's daughter. He began (reluctantly) taking on support obligations

### The Macintosh project

- **1979**: Jef Raskin launched the Macintosh project, envisioning a low-cost, easy-to-use computer
- **1981**: after being pushed off the Lisa project, Jobs took over Macintosh and redefined it as a product that would "change the world"
- **1983**: Jobs convinced Pepsi president John Sculley to join Apple as CEO, using the famous line: "Do you want to sell sugar water for the rest of your life, or do you want to come with me and change the world?"
- **January 22, 1984**: Ridley Scott's "1984" ad aired during the third quarter of Super Bowl XVIII, becoming one of the most iconic ads in advertising history
- **January 24, 1984**: the Macintosh launched officially at Apple's annual shareholders meeting at De Anza College's Flint Center. Jobs pulled the Mac out of a canvas bag, and the screen displayed "insanely great." Initial price: $2,495

### Power struggle and ouster

- **1984-1985**: Macintosh sales fell short of expectations (cooling quickly after the initial hype), and the conflict between Jobs and Sculley intensified
- **May 1985**: with Arthur Rock's backing, Sculley proposed a reorganization plan that would move Jobs out of the Macintosh division
- **May 24, 1985**: Apple's board voted to back Sculley's reorganization plan, stripping Jobs of all operational responsibilities
- **September 17, 1985**: Jobs submitted his resignation letter to Apple's board. He took 5 senior Apple employees with him and announced he was founding a new company

**A turning point in his thinking**: being pushed out of the company he founded was the biggest blow of Jobs's life, but he would later call it "the best thing that ever happened to me" — it gave him back the freedom of an entrepreneur and a beginner's mind.

## Chapter Three: The "wilderness" years (1985-1997)

### NeXT Computer

- **September 1985**: founded NeXT, Inc., aiming to build high-end workstations for the higher-education and business markets
- **October 12, 1988**: the NeXT Computer (later called the NeXT Cube) launched at Davies Symphony Hall in San Francisco. Priced at $6,500 (more without a monitor), it packed the most advanced technology of its time
- NeXT's true legacy became its operating system, NeXTSTEP — object-oriented programming, the Objective-C language, and its development tools. Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web on a NeXT machine
- **1990**: launched the NeXTstation at a lower price, but it still couldn't break into the mass market
- **1993**: NeXT stopped producing hardware (having sold only about 50,000 units total) and pivoted to a pure software company, focused on NeXTSTEP/Intel
- **1994**: NeXT turned its first annual profit ($1.03 million)

### Pixar and the animation revolution

- **February 3, 1986**: Jobs bought Lucasfilm's Computer Graphics Division from George Lucas for $5 million, and invested another $5 million, becoming Pixar's chairman and majority shareholder
- **1986**: Pixar's short film *Luxo Jr.* was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Animated Short, showcasing the potential of computer animation
- **1988**: *Tin Toy* became the first computer-animated film to win the Academy Award for Best Animated Short
- **May 1991**: Pixar signed a deal with Disney to co-produce three computer-animated feature films
- **March 18, 1991**: Jobs married Laurene Powell at the Ahwahnee Hotel in Yosemite National Park, officiated by his Zen teacher Kobun Chino Otogawa. The two had met at a talk Jobs gave at Stanford's business school
- **September 1991**: eldest son Reed Paul Jobs was born
- **1995**: daughter Erin Siena Jobs was born
- **November 22, 1995**: *Toy Story* was released, becoming the first ever fully computer-animated feature film, grossing $373 million worldwide
- **November 29, 1995**: Pixar went public on NASDAQ under the ticker "PIXR" at $22 a share; within the first half hour it climbed from $28 to $45, surpassing Netscape as the biggest IPO of the year. Jobs's Pixar shares were worth roughly $1.2 billion
- **February 25, 1997**: Pixar signed a new 10-year, 5-film agreement with Disney, splitting profits 50/50 and giving both brands equal billing

**A turning point in his thinking**: the wilderness years were the crucial period in which Jobs matured into a seasoned leader. At NeXT he learned to stop chasing "insanely great" while ignoring commercial reality; at Pixar he learned to let professionals (John Lasseter, Ed Catmull) do what they were best at. Laurene and his children gave his life an anchor.

### Changes in family relationships

- **late 1980s-1990s**: Jobs gradually built a relationship with his daughter Lisa, who moved in with Jobs and Laurene for a time
- **1998**: youngest daughter Eve Jobs was born
- The relationship remained complicated throughout: Lisa Brennan-Jobs's 2018 memoir *Small Fry* revealed the tension and eventual reconciliation between father and daughter

## Chapter Four: The return to Apple (1997-2007)

### Saving a company on the brink of bankruptcy

- **December 20, 1996**: Apple announced it was acquiring NeXT for $429 million. Jobs returned to Apple as an "informal adviser," with Gil Amelio as CEO at the time
- **July 9, 1997**: Apple's board removed Amelio as CEO
- **September 16, 1997**: Jobs was appointed interim CEO (jokingly called "iCEO"), with a symbolic salary of $1 a year
- **1997**: launched the "Think Different" ad campaign, paying tribute to "the crazy ones" — Einstein, Gandhi, Picasso, and others. This wasn't just advertising, it was a declaration that Apple's spirit had returned
- **August 6, 1997**: at Macworld Boston, announced that Microsoft would invest $150 million in Apple, drawing boos from the crowd — but the deal helped Apple through its financial crisis
- **1997-1998**: dramatically trimmed the product line, killing off Newton, Cyberdog, OpenDoc, and other projects. Simplified Apple's dozens of product lines into a four-quadrant matrix: consumer/pro x desktop/portable
- **November 10, 1997**: launched the Apple Online Store, letting consumers directly configure and buy Apple products

### The iMac and a design renaissance

- **January 8, 1998**: Apple announced it had returned to profitability
- **May 6, 1998**: launched the iMac G3 — a translucent, candy-colored all-in-one computer, designed by Jony Ive. This was the first iconic product of Jobs's return
- **August 15, 1998**: the iMac went on sale, becoming Apple's fastest-selling product ever. It redefined the idea that a computer could be beautiful and fun
- **1999**: launched a colorful iMac lineup (5 colors), along with the Power Mac G3 for professional users
- **June 1999**: launched the iBook, a consumer laptop

### The digital-hub strategy

- **January 5, 2000**: unveiled the Mac OS X strategy at Macworld — an entirely new operating system built on NeXTSTEP technology. Jobs formally changed his title from "interim CEO" to CEO
- **March 24, 2001**: Mac OS X officially launched
- **May 19, 2001**: the first Apple Retail Stores opened simultaneously in Tysons Corner, Virginia, and Glendale, California. Analysts widely predicted failure — they turned out to be badly wrong
- **October 23, 2001**: launched the original iPod — "1,000 songs in your pocket." Priced at $399, and initially Mac-only
- **January 2001**: launched the iTunes software (Mac version)
- **April 28, 2003**: the iTunes Music Store went live, selling 1 million songs in its first week, proving people would pay for music. The 99-cent-per-song pricing model upended the entire music industry
- **October 2003**: iTunes for Windows launched, opening the iPod ecosystem to PC users

### The iPod's dominance

- **January 2004**: the iPod mini launched — smaller, more stylish, and cheaper ($249)
- **January 2005**: the iPod shuffle launched (no screen, $99)
- **September 2005**: the iPod nano replaced the iPod mini, with an ultra-thin design
- **October 2005**: launched a video-capable iPod, alongside a video version of the iTunes Store
- **2006**: the iPod held roughly 75% of the US digital-music-player market

### The merger with Disney

- **January 24, 2006**: Disney announced an all-stock acquisition of Pixar for $7.4 billion. Jobs became Disney's largest individual shareholder (roughly a 7% stake) and joined its board

**A turning point in his thinking**: the Jobs who returned was a completely different person from the one who left in 1985. He had learned: (1) focus — cutting 90% of the product line; (2) collaborating with top talent rather than fighting them; (3) design isn't decoration, it's the soul of the product; (4) the importance of the retail experience. The unbroken string of successes from the iMac to the iPod proved his transformation.

## Chapter Five: The peak years (2007-2011)

### The iPhone — reinventing the phone

- **January 9, 2007**: Jobs unveiled the iPhone at the Macworld conference with his famous opening line: "Today, Apple is going to reinvent the phone." He described the iPhone as three things in one: a widescreen iPod, a revolutionary phone, and a breakthrough internet device
- **June 29, 2007**: the original iPhone launched in the US, priced at $499 (4GB)/$599 (8GB). Customers lined up around the block outside Apple Stores
- **July 11, 2008**: the iPhone 3G launched, with the price dropped to $199, supporting 3G networks

### The App Store — creating a new economy

- **July 10, 2008**: the App Store launched with an initial 500 apps, creating an entirely new software-distribution and developer economy
- The App Store logged over 10 million downloads in its first weekend
- By 2011, the App Store had over 500,000 apps and more than 18 billion total downloads

### The MacBook Air and the evolution of the laptop

- **January 15, 2008**: Jobs pulled the MacBook Air out of a standard manila envelope, announcing it as "the world's thinnest notebook"

### The iPad — defining a new category

- **January 27, 2010**: Jobs unveiled the iPad at San Francisco's Yerba Buena Center
- **April 3, 2010**: the iPad went on sale, selling over 300,000 units on day one. Before this, the tablet category had been widely seen as doomed to fail
- **June 7, 2010**: the iPhone 4 launched, introducing the Retina display
- **March 2, 2011**: Jobs personally appeared at the iPad 2 launch (while already on medical leave) — his last major product launch

### iCloud and the ecosystem

- **June 6, 2011**: Jobs unveiled iCloud at WWDC, his final public keynote. He came back from medical leave specifically to launch it, demonstrating his foresight into the future of cloud computing

### Apple's headquarters

- **June 7, 2011**: Jobs made his last public appearance, at a Cupertino city council meeting, presenting the design for Apple Park (Apple Campus 2), which he compared to "a landed spaceship." He never saw it completed

## Chapter Six: Health and legacy (2003-2011 and beyond)

### Cancer diagnosis and treatment

- **October 2003**: a routine kidney CT scan unexpectedly found a pancreatic tumor. Doctors initially believed it was ordinary pancreatic cancer (usually fatal), but it was later diagnosed as a rare pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor (pNET), which is curable through surgery
- **October 2003-July 2004 (roughly 9 months)**: Jobs refused surgery and tried alternative therapies — a vegan diet, acupuncture, herbal remedies, spiritual healing. This decision is now widely believed to have possibly delayed the optimal window for treatment
- **July 31, 2004**: underwent a pancreaticoduodenectomy (a Whipple procedure) at Stanford University Medical Center, removing part of his pancreas, gallbladder, stomach, and small intestine
- **August 1, 2004**: emailed Apple employees, telling them he had "a very rare form of pancreatic cancer," and that the surgery had been successful, requiring no chemotherapy or radiation
- **June 12, 2005**: delivered his famous Stanford commencement address, "Stay Hungry, Stay Foolish." He told three stories — connecting the dots, love and loss, and death. This became one of the most-quoted speeches of the internet era

### Declining health

- **mid-2008**: Jobs appeared noticeably thinner, sparking widespread public speculation about his health. Apple called it "a common bug"
- **January 5, 2009**: Jobs announced his "hormone imbalance" was more complex than expected
- **January 14, 2009**: Jobs announced medical leave, with Tim Cook taking over day-to-day operations
- **April 2009**: underwent a liver transplant at Methodist University Hospital in Memphis
- **late June 2009**: Jobs returned to the Apple offices

### The final years

- **January 17, 2011**: Jobs announced his second medical leave, with no set return date, and Tim Cook again took over operations
- **March 2, 2011**: returned from leave to host the iPad 2 launch (his last product launch)
- **June 6, 2011**: delivered his final WWDC keynote, launching iCloud
- **June 7, 2011**: made his final public appearance, presenting the Apple Park plan to Cupertino's city council
- **August 24, 2011**: formally resigned as Apple's CEO, writing to the board: "I have always said that if there ever came a day when I could no longer meet my duties and expectations as Apple's CEO, I would be the first to let you know. Unfortunately, that day has come." Tim Cook took over as CEO, and Jobs became chairman of the board

### Death

- **October 5, 2011**: Steve Jobs died at his home in Palo Alto, California, of respiratory arrest related to metastatic pancreatic cancer, at age 56. His wife Laurene, his children, and his sisters were by his side
- His last words (as described by his sister Mona Simpson at his memorial): "Oh wow. Oh wow. Oh wow."
- **October 16, 2011**: Apple held an employee memorial at its Cupertino headquarters
- **October 19, 2011**: a private funeral was held at Stanford's Memorial Church

### Posthumous impact and legacy

- **2012**: Walter Isaacson's authorized biography, *Steve Jobs*, was published (Jobs cooperated with interviews while alive but asked that it be published only after his death)
- **2013**: construction began on Apple Park (opened in 2017)
- **2015**: the film *Steve Jobs* was released (written by Aaron Sorkin, directed by Danny Boyle, starring Michael Fassbender)
- **2018**: daughter Lisa Brennan-Jobs published her memoir *Small Fry*, revealing the complexity of their father-daughter relationship
- **2022**: Laurene Powell Jobs's Emerson Collective became one of the world's most influential philanthropic and investment organizations
- In the decade after Jobs's death, Apple's market cap grew from roughly $350 billion to over $3 trillion, making it the most valuable company in the world

## Appendix: a summary of turning points in his thinking

1. **Being adopted** (1955) -> the belief he was "specially chosen," a root of his drive
2. **Dropping out of Reed College** (1973) -> following curiosity rather than a prescribed path
3. **The trip to India and Zen practice** (1974) -> the philosophical foundation of simplicity, intuition, and focus
4. **Visiting Xerox PARC** (1979) -> the GUI is the future, user experience matters more than technical specs
5. **Being pushed out of Apple** (1985) -> "the best thing that ever happened to me," a chance at rebirth
6. **Pixar and family** (1986-1997) -> learning to let go, trust a team, and balance a life
7. **Returning to Apple** (1997) -> the power of focus — "saying no" matters more than "saying yes"
8. **His cancer diagnosis** (2003) -> "remembering you are going to die is the best way I know to avoid the trap of thinking you have something to lose"
9. **The Stanford speech** (2005) -> Stay Hungry, Stay Foolish — a public declaration of his life philosophy
10. **The iPhone launch** (2007) -> proof that one person could redefine an entire industry more than once
